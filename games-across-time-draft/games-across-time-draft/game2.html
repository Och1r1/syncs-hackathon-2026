How it is played
  1. Move one piece one step along a drawn line to an empty point. Diagonals exist only on the “strong” points where the diagonal lines meet.
  2. Approach: step toward an enemy piece and every enemy piece in an unbroken line beyond it is captured.
  3. Withdrawal: step away from an enemy piece and the unbroken enemy line behind you is captured instead.
  4. Capturing is compulsory. If a capture exists anywhere, a quiet move (paika) is illegal.
  5. After a capture the same piece may keep capturing — but never twice in the same direction in a row, and never onto a point it already stood on this turn. Stopping early is allowed.
  6. Strip the opponent of every piece to win.

Fanorona was played on carved wooden boards in the Merina court, where royal diviners read the board as an omen before battle. The nine-column tsivy board is the tournament form; smaller telo and dimy boards were used for quick games.
